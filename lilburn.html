Why Lilburn Homes Take On Water

Lilburn sits in the middle of Gwinnett County creek country — Jackson Creek, Sweetwater Creek and Camp Creek all run through or near the city, and they rise fast when heavy rain stalls over the metro.

The National Weather Service has issued flood warnings for Sweetwater Creek near Lilburn, where flood stage is 11 feet. At that level the Weather Service reports minor flooding at the Northwood Country Club golf course area and in the woodlands downstream toward the Yellow River. When the creeks run high, low-lying streets and ground floors near the channels are the first to take water.

Beyond the creeks, sustained rain can pond against foundations and seep into basements and crawlspaces — Georgia’s red clay drains slowly, so water lingers where grading slopes toward the house, and the hidden moisture turns into a mold problem weeks later. Add the usual indoor failures, burst supply lines and failed water heaters, and you have the full picture of what local contractors get called for in the 30047.

Water Damage Restoration FAQs: Lilburn, Georgia

Common questions from homeowners in Lilburn — answered plainly.

Does homeowners insurance cover creek flooding in Lilburn?

It depends on how the water got in. Sudden and accidental damage — a burst pipe, a failed appliance hose — is often covered. Rising water from a creek or floodwater from outside usually needs a separate flood policy. Document everything with photos and notify your insurer early — the insurer, not the contractor, decides what is covered.

Who do I call for a sewer backup in Lilburn?

Start with Gwinnett County Water Resources’ 24/7 dispatch line at (678) 376-7000 — and stop using water (dishwashers, showers, laundry) until the cause is found. The county sends an investigator to check whether the blockage is in its section of the system; a blockage in your lateral outside the county right-of-way is the homeowner’s to fix. For cleanup of what the backup left behind, call (678) 833-0791 and we’ll route you to an independent restoration contractor. See the full breakdown: who to call for a sewer backup in Gwinnett County.

How fast does mold grow after a leak?

In Georgia’s humidity, mold can start growing on damp drywall, wood, and fabric within 24 to 48 hours. That short window is why fast drying matters more than almost anything else. If materials stay wet beyond a day or two, ask the contractor to check for mold during the assessment.
