How it happens

How an Attached Hose Splits a “Frost-Free” Spigot

A frost-free spigot (also called a sillcock or hose bib) shuts the water off deep inside the wall, where it’s warmer, and lets the outer part of the barrel drain when you close it. A hose left connected stops that water from draining. When it freezes, the trapped water expands and can split the barrel.

SupplyHouse describes the usual pattern: winter hits, the water trapped in the barrel expands, the tube splits, and the first use in spring sends water into the wall cavity. They note that most failures happen when a hose stays attached.

Signs of a hidden leak

What to Look For

Run the spigot briefly with someone watching inside the house, on the other side of that wall.

01Weak flow outside

Less water than usual at the hose end while the spigot is open can mean water is escaping somewhere else.

02Water inside when it runs

Dripping or a damp spot on the inside wall, baseboard, basement or crawl space only while the spigot is on.

03Stains and smells

A water stain, bubbling paint, or a musty smell near where the spigot passes through the wall.

Why it matters

A Wet Wall You Can’t See

Water inside a wall cavity soaks insulation and the back of the drywall where nobody sees it. The U.S. EPA lists the back side of drywall and the areas inside walls around leaking pipes as places mold can hide, and says water-damaged areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours.

Spigot Leak FAQs

Isn’t a frost-free spigot supposed to prevent this?

It does, as long as the water can drain out of the barrel. A hose, splitter or timer left attached keeps the water in, and then it can freeze and split like any other pipe.

How do I know if my spigot is frost-free?

A frost-free spigot usually has a longer body that reaches back into the wall, and the handle sits farther from where the water shuts off. If you aren’t sure, a plumber can tell you quickly.

Can I just replace the spigot myself?

Replacing it is a plumbing job, and the bigger question is how much water got into the wall. Have the wall checked for moisture before it’s closed back up.
