What Causes Backups in Gwinnett Homes

Backups have causes, and the causes are fixable. These are the culprits behind most sewage backups in and around Lawrenceville ZIP 30044.

01Tree roots

Georgia’s clay soil and thirsty trees push roots into pipe joints and cracks — the region’s signature sewer problem.

02Grease buildup

Fats, oils, and grease solidify inside cool pipes, narrowing them layer by layer until flow stops.

03“Flushable” wipes

They don’t break down like toilet paper — they’re a leading cause of household clogs and municipal blockages.

04Bellied lines

Sagging pipe sections collect solids until they dam the line — a camera inspection finds what plungers can’t.

05Heavy rain

Stormwater infiltrating cracked lines overwhelms capacity — if trouble always follows rain, the line is suspect.

06Aging clay tile

Old lines with many joints shift and snag debris — each joint is a future blockage point.

Sewage Backup FAQs: Lawrenceville, Georgia

Common questions from homeowners in Lawrenceville and Gwinnett County — answered plainly.

Is a sewage backup dangerous?

Yes. Sewage may carry harmful bacteria and pathogens — that’s what separates a backup from ordinary flooding. Avoid contact with backup water, keep children and pets away, and don’t attempt cleanup of significant backups yourself.

Can I clean up a sewage backup myself?

A small, contained toilet overflow on a hard surface can be disinfected by a homeowner with proper protection. Anything beyond that — multiple fixtures, soaked carpet or drywall, standing sewage — needs professionals with the right equipment and protection.

Why does sewage come up in my shower when I flush?

That’s the textbook symptom of a main-line blockage: wastewater can’t reach the street, so it takes the lowest available exit — usually a shower or tub drain. Stop using water and call a professional.

Will it happen again?

Until the cause — roots, grease, a bellied section, a broken pipe — is cleared or repaired, yes. A camera inspection finds what plungers and drain cleaners can’t, and it’s how repeat backups get prevented.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup?

Many standard policies exclude sewer backup unless you carry a specific sewer-backup rider. Check your policy now — and document any backup with photos either way. The insurer, not the contractor, decides what is covered.
